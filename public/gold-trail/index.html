Evidence still needed

  • NSW Seamless Geology maps surface rock units and interpreted faults at varying scales. Rock-unit details can be queried at the map centre. Local ground truth, alteration and veins need further investigation.
  • Geochemistry and assays: sample location, detection limit and lab QA are required.
  • Magnetics and radiometrics are indirect context, not gold detection. Gravity is not integrated. Phone magnetometers do not detect buried gold.
  • Terrain imagery shows elevation patterns. Palaeochannels, stream transport and satellite interpretation are not integrated and require field validation.
  • Metal detection and systematic panning: field checks, subject to access and permitted methods.

Sand and alluvial context

Sand and gravel may be deposited by rivers and streams. Some placer deposits contain gold, but visible sediment, dark grains, colour, a bend or a bar alone cannot establish mineral identity or gold. Look at sediment layers and bedrock context; confirm conditions and use permitted sampling where appropriate. Grain size cannot be established from an unscaled photo. Grain size, shape and water flow affect how particles move; the camera cannot see buried layers or assess safety. Avoid disturbing historic mine waste.

What are mapped faults?

Faults are mapped breaks or zones where rock has moved. These lines are geological interpretations, sometimes inferred or approximate; they do not show gold or grant access. Near the approximate Bellbrook map centre, GSNSW records the Parrabel Fault, Taylors Arm Fault system and Threadneedle Fault. Their mapped traces are approximate in these records; check the source map before using them in the field.

Fossicking rights and permissions
Access at every lead is unverified. Check land status, current closures and all required consents before entering or fossicking. A gold record, map or GPS position is not permission.

Most NSW State forests require a Forestry Corporation authorisation. Excluded areas, closures, leases and other restrictions still apply. Check the current price on the official site before applying.

Crown land
Some Crown land may be fossicked without permission, but managed, leased or licensed land has different requirements. Check the exact land status and contact Crown Lands about consent before visiting. Other title and native title requirements may also apply.
