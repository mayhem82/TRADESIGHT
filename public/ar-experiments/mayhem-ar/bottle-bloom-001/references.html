Growth observed frame by frame

The animation is an original 3D interpretation of time-lapse movement. It combines seedling growth and flower-opening observations, adapted to fit through a glass bottle. It is not an automatic 3D reconstruction or a claim that these plants grow in empty bottles.

  • Lima Bean Time Lapse — David Marvin, CC BY 3.0. Sampled 12 frames across the 119-second video. Observed: curved shoot straightens, folded leaf blades separate, then broaden and lower as the stem extends.
  • Lithops flower bud growth and first bloom — yellowcloud, CC BY 2.0. Sampled 12 frames across 34.467 seconds. Observed: a compact elongated bud grows before petals release and spread.
  • Sunflower Flower Opening Time Lapse — Derek Ramsey, GFDL 1.2. Sampled 12 frames across 35.033 seconds. Observed: curled ray petals release at different times and gradually flatten away from the centre.
  • Die Sonnenblume im Zeitraffer — Naturenow, CC BY-SA 4.0. Examined 11 of 401 frames for early shoot extension. The final image still has the seed coat; the leaf stage uses the separate lima-bean reference.

The petal and leaf surface atlas was created with OpenAI image generation using a leaf-unfurl frame from David Marvin's CC BY 3.0 lima-bean video and a flower-opening frame from yellowcloud's CC BY 2.0 Lithops video, credited above. The generated atlas supplies fine veins, cells and colour variation to the original 3D geometry; it is not a photographic scan. See texture provenance and changes.

Source footage and extracted reference frames are not included in the AR page. Geometry and animation are authored in code. Three.js r164 is bundled under its MIT license. Bottle shapes are reconstructed from the supplied photos; dimensions are estimates. World alignment is manual and lasts for the current AR session.
